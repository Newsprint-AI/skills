FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

Quillfeather Labs Raises $12 Million to Add CT and MRI Scanners to Its Equipment Failure Predictions

PITTSBURGH, Oct. 6, 2026 -- Quillfeather Labs, which makes software that predicts which hospital infusion pumps and ventilators will fail, has raised $12 million in Series A funding to move into imaging equipment: CT and MRI scanners, starting in 2027.

Harrow Point Ventures led the round. Mill Race Capital and existing seed investor Tessellate Fund took part. Quillfeather has raised $15.5 million to date. Daniel Okafor, a partner at Harrow Point, joins Quillfeather's board.

"We looked at about a dozen predictive maintenance companies," Okafor said. "Quillfeather was the only one whose customers renewed without being asked, every one of them."

Quillfeather's software reads a hospital's equipment maintenance logs and predicts which infusion pumps and ventilators will fail in the next 30 days. The company started with infusion pumps in 2022 and added ventilators in 2025. Today 41 U.S. hospitals use it, up from nine a year ago.

When a pump fails mid-shift, nurses scramble for a replacement and patients wait. Biomedical engineering teams are short-staffed and cannot inspect everything on schedule, so they need to know where to look first. The nine hospitals that have used Quillfeather for a full year had 31% fewer surprise equipment failures in that year than in the year before, according to Quillfeather's analysis of their service records, 2025.

The round also pays to double the engineering team from 14 to about 30 people by the end of 2027 and to open a second lab.

"Biomed techs are the most overworked people in a hospital, and almost nobody knows they exist," said Priya Raman, co-founder and CEO of Quillfeather. "Our job is to tell them which few machines to look at today. Scanners are the obvious next step, because that is where the money and the waiting rooms are."

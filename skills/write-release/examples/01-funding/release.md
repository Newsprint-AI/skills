Quillfeather Labs Raises $12 Million to Predict Hospital Equipment Failures Before They Reach Patients

Harrow Point Ventures led the Series A, which brings Quillfeather Labs' total funding to $15.5 million.

- Quillfeather Labs raised $12 million in Series A funding led by Harrow Point Ventures, with Mill Race Capital and seed investor Tessellate Fund taking part, for a total of $15.5 million raised to date.

PITTSBURGH, Oct. 6, 2026 — Quillfeather Labs has raised $12 million in Series A funding led by Harrow Point Ventures to grow its engineering team and extend its failure predictions from infusion pumps and ventilators to CT and MRI scanners. The Pittsburgh company's software tells short-staffed hospital biomedical engineering teams which devices are likely to fail in the next 30 days.

When an infusion pump fails mid-shift, nurses scramble for a replacement and patients wait. Hospital biomedical engineering teams are short-staffed and cannot inspect every device on schedule, so they need to know where to look first. Quillfeather Labs reads each hospital's equipment maintenance logs and flags the infusion pumps and ventilators most likely to fail in the next 30 days.

Key figures
- $12 million Series A, led by Harrow Point Ventures, with Mill Race Capital and existing seed investor Tessellate Fund taking part
- $15.5 million raised in total to date
- 41 U.S. hospitals use Quillfeather Labs today, up from nine a year ago
- 31% fewer surprise equipment failures at the nine hospitals that have used Quillfeather Labs for a full year, compared with the year before (Quillfeather Labs analysis of the hospitals' service records, 2025)

"Biomed techs are the most overworked people in the hospital, and nobody knows they exist," said Priya Raman, co-founder and CEO of Quillfeather Labs. "Our whole job is to tell them which three machines to look at today. Scanners are the obvious next step because that's where the money and the waiting rooms are."

# Interview record: funding round

Quillfeather Labs is a fictional company. Any resemblance to a real company is coincidence.

1. **Who:** Quillfeather Labs
2. **What happened, and when:** Raised $12 million in Series A funding.
3. **Key facts (funding):** Series A. Led by Harrow Point Ventures, with Mill Race Capital and existing seed investor Tessellate Fund taking part. Total raised to date is $15.5 million. The money pays to double the engineering team from 14 to about 30 people by the end of 2027 and to open a second lab. The company started with infusion pumps in 2022 and added ventilators in 2025. This round funds the move to imaging equipment: CT and MRI scanners, starting in 2027. 41 U.S. hospitals use Quillfeather's software today, up from nine a year ago.
4. **Why it matters:** When a pump fails mid-shift, nurses scramble for a replacement and patients wait. Biomedical engineering teams are short-staffed and can't inspect everything on schedule, so they need to know where to look first.
5. **Evidence:** The nine hospitals that have used Quillfeather for a full year had 31% fewer surprise equipment failures in that year than in the year before. Source: Quillfeather's analysis of their service records, 2025.
6. **Who speaks** (no quotes written yet):
   - Priya Raman, co-founder and CEO. Her view, from the interview notes: biomed techs are the most overworked people in a hospital and almost nobody knows they exist. The product's job is to tell them which few machines to look at today. Scanners are the obvious next step because that is where the money and the waiting rooms are. She approves her own quote.
   - Daniel Okafor, partner at Harrow Point Ventures (joins the board). His view, from the notes: the firm looked at about a dozen predictive maintenance companies, and Quillfeather was the only one whose customers renewed without being asked, every one of them. Harrow Point approves his quote.
